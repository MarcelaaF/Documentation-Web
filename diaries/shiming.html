Shi Ming's Diary

Weekly reflections on building Limos.

Week 1 (3–9 July 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • Agreed on communication channels, appointed a team leader, and discussed initial responsibilities.
  • Researched competitors, discussed the project scope and infrastructure, and documented preliminary requirements.

⚠ Challenges

Coordinating responsibilities and narrowing the initial scope took discussion, particularly with a member missing during early coordination.

💡 Reflection

I spent the first week getting to know the team and the project expectations. Setting up communication channels and sharing our initial research gave us a foundation for the proposal.

Week 2 (10–16 July 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • Prepared the first draft of the project proposal, covering the background, objectives, scope, limitations, and methodology.
  • Discussed the proposal with the supervisor and began setting up the project documentation website.

⚠ Challenges

  • The project direction, objectives, and feasibility of some features were not yet clear.
  • User stories and use cases still needed clarification before responsibilities and the timetable could be finalised.

💡 Reflection

Completing the proposal gave us a shared starting point, although the purpose of the project website and some submission expectations initially needed clarification. The supervisor discussion helped guide the next steps.

Week 3 (17–23 July 2026)

Objectives Achieved

Defined system requirements and user stories, drafted use cases, and began preparing diagrams for the app's workflows.

⚠ Challenges

  • Some submission expectations, workflows, and design decisions were still unclear.
  • Changing requirements made it difficult to keep diagrams consistent across user roles.

💡 Reflection

The use cases helped make the intended interactions clearer. We also needed a better way to track completed and outstanding work, especially for documentation.

Week 4 (24–30 July 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • Continued preparing UML diagrams and discussing the app's key features and implementation.
  • Divided design work by application page and user role, prioritising core components.

⚠ Challenges

  • Keeping diagrams aligned with evolving workflows and requirements.
  • Understanding Firebase and its integration with Flutter as we began setting up the app.

💡 Reflection

The design discussions exposed possible feature creep. We needed to focus on the dietary aspects of the app and clarify the core workflows before expanding the scope.

Week 5 (31 July–6 August 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • Began wireframes and initial coding, including work on the Flutter and Firebase setup.
  • Reviewed progress with the supervisor and discussed next steps.

⚠ Challenges

  • Development environment configuration and dependencies slowed the initial setup.
  • Responsibilities and communication needed clarification under time pressure.

💡 Reflection

Getting started showed how important it was to ask about responsibilities early and keep everyone informed. Team support helped with the environment setup.

Week 6 (7–13 August 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • The team established the Android and Firebase/Firestore foundation and implemented core screens.
  • Updated Firestore rules and sequence diagrams, and began preparing test cases and user documentation.

⚠ Challenges

  • Branch synchronisation, merge conflicts, and dependency issues interrupted development.
  • Firestore API availability blocked planned data seeding, while limited documentation made existing features harder to work on.

💡 Reflection

The team was communicating more and making progress, with considerable help from Marcela. Earlier documentation and better time management would make it easier to balance development and other deadlines.

Week 7 (14–20 August 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • Continued writing and running test cases, recording results, and updating the app as needed.
  • The team fixed home-page issues and improved role-specific dashboards and navigation.

⚠ Challenges

No major new challenges were recorded, although limited documentation still made it harder to work across features.

💡 Reflection

Automated test scripts helped us check requirements more quickly. Thanks to Marcela and Augustin for their contributions; the app still needed further testing and refinement.

Week 8 (21–27 August 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • Worked on technical descriptions for the diagrams and continued testing.
  • The team redesigned administrator and business-partner pages and added API-key configuration.

⚠ Challenges

  • Design changes meant diagrams and their descriptions needed repeated updates.
  • The documentation had to remain consistent with the evolving dashboards.

💡 Reflection

Delaying clarification created avoidable complications. Reviewing the implementation and documentation together showed why we should resolve design questions sooner.

Week 9 (28 August–3 September 2026)

Objectives Achieved

  • Continued the technical design document, updated diagrams and descriptions, and worked on the user manual.
  • Continued checking features and preparing for the next testing cycle.

⚠ Challenges

Keeping the diagrams, explanations, and manual consistent required revisiting earlier decisions during a busy period.

💡 Reflection

Revisiting the documentation revealed gaps to address and provided a better starting point for the next cycle of development and testing.

Week 10 (4–10 September 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team continued testing, connected recipe search to Spoonacular, improved recipe flows, made the router redirect guard testable, and refined account deletion. Presentation preparation also began.

⚠ Challenges

External recipe search and account flows introduced dependencies that needed careful testing alongside the upcoming presentation.

💡 Reflection

Testing while refining the flows helped expose problems earlier and made it easier to explain the system's progress.

Week 11 (11–17 September 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team refined theme and unit inputs, improved account-deletion cleanup and wording, and simplified water logging.

⚠ Challenges

Account deletion needed reliable cleanup of related data, while inputs and interface text needed to remain clear.

💡 Reflection

These smaller refinements reinforced the need to check the whole user experience, including what happens when an account is deleted.

Week 12 (18–24 September 2026)

Objectives Achieved

Existing member, recipe, and partner flows were reviewed in preparation for the next iteration. No feature push is recorded for this period.

⚠ Challenges

A gap in dated pushes makes the work during this period difficult to verify from repository history alone.

💡 Reflection

More regular progress records would make it easier to track what was done and what still needs attention.

Week 13 (25 September–1 October 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team added goal-aware recipe recommendations, calorie-range ranking, and meal, water, and custom reminders. Firestore rules and tests were updated; the recorded test run reported 124 passing tests.

⚠ Challenges

Recommendations needed to respect dietary and allergy restrictions while ranking for goals, and reminders needed to stay in sync with account state.

💡 Reflection

Separating safety filters from ranking and centralising reminder behaviour made these features easier to reason about and test.

Week 14 (2–8 October 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team integrated Gemini food recognition, added iOS camera and photo-library permissions, and fixed scan review and reminder issues.

⚠ Challenges

Food scanning depended on platform permissions and validation of AI-generated values before saving.

💡 Reflection

Considering recognition, review, and saving as one workflow helped identify issues beyond the scan itself.

Week 15 (9–15 October 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team improved the scan controller and review validation, and added nutritionist browsing, profile viewing, and chat-session functionality.

⚠ Challenges

The new consultation flows needed to fit existing navigation and session state.

💡 Reflection

Connecting browsing and profiles to chat made the consultation flow clearer from a member's perspective.

Week 16 (16–22 October 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team implemented nutritionist chat requests, accept and decline actions, messaging, session ending, active-session handling, and personal recipe recommendations.

⚠ Challenges

Members and nutritionists needed consistent session state throughout requests, conversations, and session endings.

💡 Reflection

Working through the full consultation lifecycle showed that a useful chat feature needs more than a messaging screen.

Week 17 (23–29 October 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team added an AI nutrition assistant and nutritionist handoff question, replaced the meal-logging food catalog with HPB data, scaled nutrition by serving size, and improved recipe search and favourites.

⚠ Challenges

AI guidance, local food data, and web recipes used different sources and required careful handling of nutrition values and serving sizes.

💡 Reflection

A consistent food catalog improved meal logging, while the handoff option linked automated guidance with human consultation.

Week 18 (30 October–5 November 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team rebranded the app as LIMOS, moved API keys to build-time configuration, refreshed welcome and authentication screens, and introduced Premium access, PDPA consent, and a seven-day trial.

⚠ Challenges

Consent and Premium restrictions needed to fit the sign-up and access flows without confusing existing members.

💡 Reflection

Configuration, consent, and subscription access all needed to be considered as part of the overall product experience.

Week 19 (6–14 November 2026)

Objectives Achieved

The team redesigned progress views and added subscription choices, password recovery, email verification, recipe editing, nutrition validation, and administrator tools. Further fixes addressed slow requests, offline meal saves, chat pagination, Firestore stream caching, role-card login errors, and Android release network access.

⚠ Challenges

Testing exposed reliability and access-control edge cases, including slow or offline requests and incorrect role selection across several flows.

💡 Reflection

The final recorded work emphasised stability and safeguards alongside new features. Release behaviour still needs verification on target devices.
